I do SEO full time

I've been doing SEO for a year and a half. I'm the SEO specialist at a marketing agency in Rexburg. One of my accounts is a national payments processor, and the rest are local businesses.

A national payments processor

My enterprise account is a national payments processor and its co-branded programs. Their own teams put my work live, so I don't name the company here.

  • Keyword architecture

    I mapped every indexable page to one target keyword. Each URL got a call: keep, rewrite, merge, redirect or retire.

  • Schema and metadata at scale

    Every indexable page on the account got a JSON-LD graph and a new title and description. I validated all of it before handing it over.

  • An orphaned article library

    A whole article section had no path a crawler could follow. The category controls were buttons, and Googlebot doesn't click buttons.

  • One page type a week

    The content plan takes one page type each week. I build its template once, and then it goes out to every page of that type.

  • Partner bank audits

    I score a bank's public website against a fixed framework, then write the gap analysis and a roadmap. The same crawl runs again for the next partner.

  • Support center rebuilds

    I pitched these as working mockups instead of slides. They used the real content inside the real header and footer.

Local businesses

On the local side I've worked with Bravo 6 Flight Academy, Outdoors for Youth and Madison Health. I rebuilt the first two. Madison Health kept its site, and I do the SEO on it there.

  • A page for each town

    Bravo 6 trains pilots in Rexburg and Pocatello, so each town has its own page. The schema on each page has that location's hours and coordinates, and it names the airport.

  • Off Wix with every slug kept

    On Wix, the camp's content was stuck inside iframes. I rebuilt the site by hand and matched every slug to the old one, so the rankings carried over.

  • Off Divi

    Bravo 6 was a Divi site on WordPress. Now my Python scripts build it as 165 static pages with clean URLs. It moved onto the main domain in August.

What I check on every site

I wrote my own crawler. It checks status codes, whether each page can be indexed, canonicals, schema types and redirect chains, against every sitemap the site lists.

I measure titles and descriptions in pixels, because Google cuts them off by width.

Then I read the schema by hand. A graph can pass validation and still describe the wrong thing.
