A builder caps what you can do for search

GoDaddy's builder decided the schema, the sitemap and the titles, and got all three wrong. None of it was editable from inside the builder.

The business schema pointed at a made-up domain, the sitemap carried junk URLs, and the titles came out double-encoded. Every one of those is a search problem the platform will not let you fix.

So the rebuild was about getting control back: fourteen hand-coded pages from her own copy and prices, her photos compressed and self-hosted, one schema graph per page. All of it editable now, which was the point.

Tarmac

Sign-in required

tarmac.hangarmarketing.com

Staff time and operations, including the timeclock: an iPad kiosk, a phone portal and an admin panel. Built on Firebase, later moved to AWS Amplify.

  • PTO accrues per pay period and holidays credit that day's scheduled hours.
  • Payroll export separates worked hours from paid hours.
  • Face clock-in at the kiosk is opt-in, runs on the device, and no image leaves the iPad.

Enterprise Mockups

Sign-in required

enterprisemockups.web.app

The client review board. A canvas where each mockup is a frame and reviewers pin comments to the exact spot. The export names the file, the position and the element, so a fix can be handed straight to whoever is doing it.

  • Frames can be PDFs, HTML files, images, live URLs, or a zipped multi-page site.
  • Pins are stored as fractions of the frame, so they hold at any zoom and survive the file being replaced.

There is a design editor inside it too: snapping guides, groups, align and distribute, a component library and live multi-cursor presence.

Deleting archives rather than destroys. Purging is the only action that removes anything for good, and the privacy page says so.

04 Enterprise

SEO at national scale

One account, a national payments processor and its co-branded programs. Research, recommendations and working mockups their own teams take live, so I do not name it here.

  • Keyword architecture

    Every indexable page mapped to one target keyword, with a keep, rewrite, merge, redirect or retire call on each URL.

  • Resource and support center rebuilds

    Pitches delivered as working mockups instead of slides, built from the client's real content inside their real header and footer.

  • Schema and metadata at scale

    A JSON-LD graph plus a rewritten title and description for every indexable page across the account, validated before handover.

  • Fixing an orphaned library

    A whole article section had no crawlable path into it, because the controls that opened each category were buttons rather than links. Googlebot follows links and does not click buttons.

  • Content plan restructure

    One page type per week per account. The template gets built once and rolls across every page of that type, instead of a page picked at random each week.

  • Partner bank web audits

    Scoring a bank's public web presence against a fixed framework, delivered as a scorecard, gap analysis and roadmap. The crawl rig is re-runnable for the next partner.

  • Personalized marketing toolkit

    A marketing page for merchants. A code in the URL swaps in that merchant's own QR code and matches the downloadable assets to their account.

So I build sites that look like someone cared: real type, real spacing, the client's own photos rather than stock, and pages that hold together on a phone as well as they do on a monitor. Then I do the search work underneath, because a beautiful site nobody can find is a brochure in a drawer.

The other half of it is that when a tool I need does not exist, I build it. The project management app exists because the agency outgrew Asana. The crawler exists because I wanted every page's title width, canonical and schema in one table. Most of what is further up this page started that way.

06 Questions

Common questions

Every site I build is one hundred percent hand-coded, and I move sites off page builders onto that. What I do not do is work inside a page builder.

Builds start at $1,500. Bigger projects cost more, and you get the number before you commit to anything.

Do I own the site afterward?
Yes. You get the files, and the hosting and domain stay in your name.
I am on Wix or Squarespace. Do I have to move?
To work with me, yes. I build hand-coded sites and I migrate sites onto them, but I do not take on work inside a page builder. Your URLs, wording and rankings come across with you.
Can you work on my existing site instead of rebuilding it?
If it is hand-coded or WordPress, yes. Audits, schema, metadata and speed work all happen in place. If it is on a builder, the audit still stands on its own, but the fixes come with the rebuild.
What do you need from me to start?
The URL. Access to Search Console and Analytics sharpens the priorities if you have them, but a crawl needs neither, so the first look can start with the URL alone.
