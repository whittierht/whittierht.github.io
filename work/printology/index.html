The whole shop, rebuilt from scratch

Printology is a print shop with two locations in Utah, and their site is on Shopify. I'm rebuilding all of it from scratch: the store customers see, and a back office for the staff. None of it is live yet.

Printing is priced by the sheet. The calculator starts at one sheet and draws every sheet size to scale. The order form shows a live estimate from fixed rules, so the same job always gets the same price.

Customers can upload files up to 500 MB and approve an itemized quote. Each order has its own page with a six-digit code. That's where the customer follows the status and approves the proof.

There's a shop too, with collections, sales and codes, and guest checkout for pickup. The old Shopify addresses are kept or redirected.

On October 10 I loaded both home pages on a phone-sized screen. The live one made 267 requests and weighed 12.1 MB. Mine made 40 requests and weighed 1.2 MB.

Payments, email, texts and shipping are built and running in test mode. They're waiting on the shop's own accounts.

What the staff get

The back office has 50 screens. Orders, appointments, prices and the site's pages are all in there, along with the time clock and the monthly email.

Staff clock in at a PIN kiosk. Every change goes into a history with undo, and backups run every night.

Every page has a feedback button. You drag a box around the spot you mean.

The Studio is 30 tools for the print counter, like Sheet maker, Passport photos and Numbered tickets. Each one makes a file on the computer in front of you and uploads nothing.

The design editor in that list is OpenDesign, an open-source project. I built the other 29.
